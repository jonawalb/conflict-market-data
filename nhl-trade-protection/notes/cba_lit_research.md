## PART 1: CBA institutions

### 1. Article 11.8: "Individually Negotiated Limitations on Player Movement" (VERIFIED-PRIMARY)
Source: NHL–NHLPA CBA, Feb 15, 2013 (2012–2022 agreement), pp. 47–48. https://media.nhl.com/site/asset/public/ext/CBA.pdf (local text extract in scratchpad/dl/cba2013.txt, lines 2878–2908)

Verbatim:
> **11.8(a)** The SPC of any Player who is a Group 3 Unrestricted Free Agent under Article 10.1(a) may contain a no-Trade or a no-move clause. SPCs containing a no-Trade or a no-move clause may be entered into prior to the time that the Player is a Group 3 Unrestricted Free Agent so long as the SPC containing the no-Trade or no-move clause extends through and does not become effective until the time that the Player qualifies for Group 3 Unrestricted Free Agency. If the Player is Traded or claimed on Waivers prior to the no-Trade or no-move clause taking effect, the clause does not bind the acquiring Club. An acquiring Club may agree to continue to be bound by the no-Trade or no-move clause, which agreement shall be evidenced in writing to the Player, Central Registry and the NHLPA, in accordance with Exhibit 3 hereof.

> **11.8(b)** A no-Trade clause or a no-move clause that is negotiated as part of an extension of an SPC entered into pursuant to Section 50.5(f) may become effective immediately upon registration of, but prior to the effective date of, such SPC extension, provided: (i) the Player would otherwise have been eligible as of the immediately preceding July 1 prior to signing the SPC extension to have a no-Trade or no-move clause pursuant to Section 11.8; and (ii) the Club and the Player ... agree that the no-Trade or no-move clause is effective immediately upon execution of the SPC extension (or at some later date agreed to by the Club and the Player) and evidence such agreement in writing in the SPC.

> **11.8(c)** A no-move clause may prevent the involuntary relocation of a Player, whether by Trade, Loan or Waiver claim. A no-move clause, however, may not restrict the Club's Buy-Out and termination rights as set forth in this Agreement. Prior to exercising its Ordinary Course Buy-Out rights ... the Club shall ... provide the Player with the option of electing to be placed on Waivers. [24-hour election window]

Article 10.1(a)(i) (eligibility threshold, verbatim):
> Any Player who either has seven (7) Accrued Seasons or is 27 years of age or older as of June 30 of the end of a League Year, shall, if his most recent SPC has expired ... become an Unrestricted Free Agent.

Section 13.21(a): before a contract is terminated via unconditional waivers, "a Player with a no-move clause shall have the rights set forth in Section 11.8."

**Corrections and nuances for the paper:**
- Eligibility is Group 3 UFA status: 7 accrued seasons, or age 27 or older as of June 30. A clause can be signed earlier, but only if it takes effect once the player reaches UFA eligibility (11.8(a)). On an extension, it can take effect immediately if the player was already eligible as of the prior July 1 (11.8(b)).
- **"Voided upon trade unless the acquiring team agrees" applies only to a clause that has not yet taken effect.** Once a clause is in effect, it stays in the SPC after a trade. The player has to waive it (fully or partially) for the trade to happen, and the acquiring club then holds a contract that still contains the clause. Practitioner sources agree, e.g. PuckPedia "Contract Clauses" (https://www.puckpedia.com/salary-cap/contract-clauses, 403 on fetch).
- NTC vs NMC. The CBA does not define the NTC beyond "no-Trade." 11.8(c) defines the NMC as blocking involuntary relocation by trade, loan (including to the minors) or waiver claim, but not a buyout. Modified NTCs (lists of teams) are a matter of contract practice and are not CBA text. Secondary descriptions: UB Sports Law Forum, Marder (2025-03-04), https://ublawsportsforum.com/2025/03/04/tackling-no-trade-and-no-movement-clauses-in-the-nhl/
- **2025 CBA (2026-27 to 2029-30):** I found no reported change to 11.8 in any coverage (Daily Faceoff, CapWages, PuckPedia summaries). **UNVERIFIED** against the 2025 MOU text, which I could not obtain. Bettman said on 2026-10-01 that "we've gotten past the way no-trade clauses were originally intended" and that the league will "keep an eye on" them, signaling the issue for the *next* CBA (Daily Faceoff, Scott Maxwell, 2026-10-01, https://www.dailyfaceoff.com/news/gary-bettman-no-trade-clauses-request-gotten-past-intended-tkachuk-larkin-hellebuyck). The NHLPA (Hainsey, Walsh) called this "an attack on players' rights" (https://www.tsn.ca/nhl/article/nhlpas-hainsey-blasts-bettmans-ntc-comments-an-attack-on-players-cba-negotiated-rights/ ; https://thescore.com/nhl/news/3616275).
- Prevalence (REPORTED, definitions differ): trade-protected contracts "surged by 480 per cent since 2013-14 when there were only 46," and "260 of 861 players who appeared this season [2024-25] have an NMC or NTC." Both are attributed to the Toronto Star via UB Sports Law Forum (2025-03-07), https://ublawsportsforum.com/2025/03/07/nhl-trade-deadline-week-why-trade-protection-is-changing-the-deadline/. Deseret News (2026-08-16): 111 NMCs and 312 players with some trade protection, https://www.deseret.com/sports/2026/08/16/nhl-no-trade-clauses/. Forbes (2026-06-27): 245, https://www.forbes.com/sites/ericmacramalla/2026/06/27/the-nhls-obsession-with-no-trade-clauses-why-hockey-is-the-outlier-in-north-american-sports/

**Expansion-draft NMC rule**
- 2017 (Vegas), VERIFIED-PRIMARY (league document): players with an NMC in effect at the time of the draft "must be protected (and will be counted toward their club's applicable protection limits)." NHL.com, 2017-06-16, https://www.nhl.com/news/nhl-expansion-draft-rules-281010592. Clubs could ask players to waive the NMC for draft purposes; the deadline to request a waiver was June 12, 2017 (REPORTED, Pension Plan Puppets, https://pensionplanpuppets.com/2017-nhl-expansion-draft-waive-nmc-no-movement-clause-marc-andre-fleury-dion-phaneuf-rules).
- 2021 (Seattle), VERIFIED: NHL.com states the Kraken draft followed "the same rules ... as the Vegas Golden Knights in 2017" (https://www.nhl.com/news/seattle-kraken-2021-nhl-expansion-draft-rules-same-as-vegas-golden-kni-302586918). The NMC-protection rule therefore carried over; the Wharton expansion paper says the same explicitly (https://wsb.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/2020_Hockey_Malter_expansion-1.pdf). Vegas was exempt in 2021.

Contracts signed before Sept 16, 2026 are under the old variance rule (25%). So **the July 1, 2026 free-agency period was still governed by the 8/7 maximums.** https://www.dailyfaceoff.com/news/new-nhl-nhlpa-cba-collective-bargaining-agreement-rule-changes-ebug-dress-code-contract-term-neck-protection ; https://www.dailyfaceoff.com/news/nhl-nhlpa-ratify-four-year-collective-bargaining-agreement-cba-2026-27 ; https://capwages.com/articles/2025-memorandum-of-understanding

Other 2025-MOU items (CapWages unless noted):
- Signing bonuses capped at 60% of total contract value (REPORTED; summary of PuckPedia/Daily Faceoff coverage; **UNVERIFIED** against the text).
- Back-loaded deals: Year-2 pay ≤ 2× Year-1 (REPORTED, Daily Faceoff citing PuckPedia).
- **Playoff salary cap.** The 20-man playoff dressed roster must be cap-compliant, starting with the 2026 playoffs (2025-26 season), which closes the LTIR playoff loophole. LTIR relief for a returning player is limited (about $3.82M); full relief applies only if the player is out for the rest of the season and playoffs (effective 2025-26).
- Ban on double retention (2025-26). Ban on deferred salary in new contracts. League minimum rises to $1.0M by 2029-30 ($850k in 2026-27, $900k, $950k).
- 84-game regular season and 4 preseason games per team, from 2026-27 (Daily Faceoff 2025-07-08).
- No NTC/NMC changes reported (see above).

### B. Academic work on NTCs, NMCs, trade requests and player-initiated trades
**Bottom line: the literature is very thin.** I found exactly **one peer-reviewed econometric study of no-trade clauses**, on MLB. I found **none estimating an NTC/NMC salary discount in the NHL**, and **no peer-reviewed economics or political-science paper on trade requests or demands** in any league. The rest is adjacent work (compensating differentials, mobility restrictions, trades vs. sales) plus a few law-review notes.
